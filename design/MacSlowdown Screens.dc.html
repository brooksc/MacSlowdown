5A measured condition is not a slowdown

The governing change lands hardest on the thing I'd assumed was settled: the summary sentence. Every screen used to open with a verdict, and there is no verdict left to give — so the opening line becomes a measurement with an interval, and the interesting design problem moves to what sits beside it. My answers: coverage is the new spine of the healthy state (5a) — not a badge but a strip along the bottom of the day, because "we watched and nothing crossed the line" is a claim about a period, and a period needs a length. Severity chips are replaced by an interval chip plus a provenance dot, and ranking is gone entirely (5h). The report gesture is one click with a second click for "a few minutes ago", and what it gives back is the thing only it can give: a marker to compare against next time (5c, 5d). One disagreement, in 5h: I think "quiet for this work session" should not expire silently.

Why coverage is a strip and not a badge "We were watching" is a claim about a period, and a period needs a length — a badge can only say yes, which is exactly the useless green light. Putting it directly under the chart makes the two read as one sentence: this is what we saw, and this is when we could see. A gap is then self-explanatory without a word of copy, because the trace and the hatching line up. It also degrades honestly: the day the app crashed at 11am, the strip is hatched from 11am and the headline can only speak for what came before.
Designed against engagement One chart, four figures, one past episode — and nothing that rewards a second look ten minutes later. The headline names the interval since anything last happened, so opening it twice in an hour gives you the same sentence with a bigger number, which is the correct disincentive. The "one thing worth knowing" line is the only concession to curiosity, and it is a fact about the day rather than a prompt. No streaks, no scores, no daily summary worth returning for.

5hWhat replaces the severity chips, and where the three confidences live
A severity chip did three jobs at once: it said how bad the measurement was, how bad the experience was, and where the episode ranked against others. The second is now forbidden and the third was never defensible — Severe and High were our words for our own thresholds, and a person reading them heard a claim about their afternoon. So the chip is replaced by an interval and a provenance dot, and ranking disappears.
Before and after
Severe Ranked our thresholds, and read as a claim about the user's day. Measured · reached critical for 5 min 40 s The state name is macOS's own (warning, critical) — a measurement, not our judgement. The interval is the only magnitude offered, because duration is the one thing a person can weigh for themselves. Still going For a live condition, whether it has ended is more useful than how large it is. You reported this A user report is a first-class record with its own provenance colour, and never inherits a severity.
The three confidences, kept apart by sentence position
The measurement — stated flatly, in the headline
"Memory pressure stayed above warning for 11 minutes." No qualifier, because there is nothing uncertain about it.
The attribution — always a separate sentence, always hedged in its verb
"Chrome held the largest measurable share" is a measurement. "Chrome was the main contributor" is a hypothesis, and it never shares a sentence with the first.
The effect on you — named as unknown, or as yours
Either "we don't know whether you noticed this" or, where a report exists, "you told us it felt slow at 3:14". There is no third option and no inferred middle.
Three dots, three colours, and no scores — a number on any of these would recombine what the wording just separated. The dots repeat the shape distinction already used for measured / calculated / likely in 1e, so this is the same vocabulary, not a second one.
Applied across the shipped screens this turn: the chips are gone from 1e and 1h; the incident list in 1f now reads as conditions with intervals rather than ranked severities, and says which ones interrupted; the CPU notification is deleted from 1g since CPU no longer interrupts, leaving the mute sheet and first run; 1e's "likely" paragraph is split into attribution and effect; the old Alerts settings and per-app rules cards are replaced by 5g and 5f. Not done, and worth a decision: first run in 1g still promises to "tell you about slowdowns", which is now the wrong promise — it should say what it watches and admit what it can't see, and I'd rather rewrite that screen properly than patch the sentence.

Positions first, so you can argue with the reasoning rather than the pixels. The Now-screen work from this turn has moved into 5b, which merges it with the earlier view. 4dMenu bar icon reconciled — the build is right, my four-colour spec was wrong
I endorse the deviation. My earlier spec — green, yellow, red and grey across four states — reasoned about the icon in isolation, where four colours look like a considerate accessibility decision. In a real menu bar it is a permanent green light next to a strip of monochrome templates — a beacon for the one state that needs no attention, and a red that appears for any open incident stops meaning anything by the third time you see it. Why we can't name it. App Store apps run in a sandbox that reports total system CPU but not per-process CPU for protected system processes — WindowServer, the Spotlight system indexer, Time Machine, audio and backup daemons. The 79% above is a real measurement of what's left over, not a rounding error. We can still see that those processes exist and when they start and stop — which is where the timing evidence above comes from. Activity Monitor is unsandboxed and can see inside.
