Positions first, so you can argue with the reasoning rather than the pixels. Run-queue pressure gets its own vocabulary — it is the first quantity in this product that is neither a rate nor a proportion, so it gets a unit nobody can misread, a graphic with no axis to mistake for 100%, and a sentence that explains why 48% CPU still feels slow (4b). Four columns, and why the number is the mean FR-059 requires the sort key to be a visible column and to come from a window rather than the newest sample. Those two together decide the column: the number is the 60-second mean, it is what orders the list, and the heading says so. 1MacSlowdown — menu bar utility, first pass

Read of the brief: the product wins or loses on triage — the shortest path from "my Mac feels slow" to a named culprit and an honest, safe next step. So the menu bar is the primary surface and the window is the evidence room. Strict macOS 26 conventions: system font, standard control metrics, sidebar-and-inspector windows, no invented chrome. Two rules run through every screen — severity is never carried by color alone (glyph + word + color), and every causal statement is tagged by how it was known (measured / calculated / likely). Non-technical users get the plain sentence at the top; power users get the raw numbers one click down, never hidden behind a mode switch.
